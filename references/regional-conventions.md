# Regional CV conventions

Look up the section for the market by its ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code. Precedence, highest first:

1. What the job posting explicitly asks for (format, language, documents, salary, photo).
2. The employer's own norms (multinational vs local firm, industry).
3. This guide.
4. The generic default at the end.

These are hiring conventions, not legal advice, and they change. When unsure, ask the user or state the assumption you made.

## Rules for every market

- Never include national ID, passport or social security numbers, bank details, or a full street address. City and country are enough.
- Sensitive personal details (date of birth or age, gender, marital status, nationality, religion, health, family details, photo) are left out by default. Include one only if the market section below says it is customary **and** the user agrees.
- Work authorisation or visa status is not a sensitive detail. Include it when it helps the application, for example when the candidate is relocating or the market cares about work passes.
- The renderer cannot place photos. If a market expects one, tell the user to add it themselves or attach it separately.
- Salary details appear only if the user wants them there or the posting asks for them on the CV.

## US — United States
- Name: résumé. Paper: US Letter. Length: 1 page under about 10 years of experience, 2 pages maximum otherwise.
- Language: American English spelling (optimize, color).
- Photo: no. Personal details: none beyond contact details. Leave out age, date of birth, marital status, nationality and religion; employers avoid them for anti-discrimination reasons.
- Common extras: work authorisation line if not a US citizen or permanent resident (for example "Requires H-1B sponsorship"). No "References available upon request".
- Dates: "Jan 2022 – Present".
- Notes: heavy ATS use (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo, SuccessFactors). Keep a concise, keyword-rich summary of 2–3 lines.

## CA — Canada
- Name: résumé. Paper: US Letter. Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: Canadian English (colour, centre, but "-ize" is common). French CV for francophone roles in Quebec.
- Photo: no. Personal details: none; same reasoning as the US.
- Common extras: work permit or permanent resident status when relevant.
- Dates: "Jan 2022 – Present".

## GB — United Kingdom
- Name: CV. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages; up to 3 for senior or academic roles.
- Language: British English (optimise, organisation).
- Photo: no. Personal details: no date of birth, age or marital status (Equality Act 2010).
- Common extras: a short personal profile at the top; right-to-work line if relevant ("Full right to work in the UK"); driving licence only if the job needs it. References are usually not listed.
- Dates: "Jan 2022 – Present".

## IE — Ireland
- Same as GB: CV, A4, 2 pages, British spelling, no photo or date of birth.
- Common extras: work permit or Stamp status for non-EEA candidates.

## DE — Germany
- Name: Lebenslauf (CV). Paper: A4. Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: German unless the posting is in English.
- Photo: traditional but optional; the General Equal Treatment Act (AGG) means employers may not require one. International firms usually prefer none.
- Personal details: date of birth and nationality were traditional and are now optional. Default: leave them out.
- Common extras: languages with CEFR levels (A1–C2); a full application often also includes a cover letter (Anschreiben) and references (Arbeitszeugnisse). A place, date and signature line at the end is optional.
- Dates: "03/2021 – heute" in German CVs.

## AT — Austria / CH — Switzerland
- Similar to Germany. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages.
- Language: the region's language (German, French or Italian in Switzerland) or English for international roles.
- Photo: still common in Switzerland and Austria; optional.
- Common extras (Switzerland): nationality and permit type (B, C, G) are commonly listed for non-Swiss candidates; references (Referenzen) are common.

## FR — France
- Name: CV. Paper: A4. Length: 1 page for early-career, 2 for experienced.
- Language: French unless the posting is in English.
- Photo: optional and declining.
- Common extras: a CV title (titre du CV) under the name; languages with CEFR levels.
- Dates: "2021 – 2023" or "mars 2021 – aujourd'hui".

## NL — Netherlands, BE — Belgium, Nordics (SE, DK, NO, FI)
- Name: CV. Paper: A4. Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: English is widely accepted, especially in tech; otherwise the local language.
- Photo: optional (somewhat common in the Nordics and Belgium).
- Common extras: short personal profile; languages with CEFR levels.

## ES — Spain / IT — Italy / PT — Portugal
- Name: CV (currículum / curriculum vitae). Paper: A4. Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: the local language unless the posting is in English.
- Photo: traditionally common; optional for multinational employers.
- Common extras (Italy): a GDPR consent line at the end is customary, for example "Autorizzo il trattamento dei miei dati personali ai sensi del Regolamento UE 2016/679 (GDPR)."

## HK — Hong Kong
- Name: CV or résumé. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages; up to 3 for senior roles.
- Language: English for most professional roles, usually British spelling. A Chinese CV (Traditional Chinese) only when the posting is in Chinese or the employer is a local Chinese-language organisation.
- Photo: optional; not expected by multinationals, sometimes expected by local firms.
- Personal details: never include the HKID number (Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance). Date of birth and marital status are not needed.
- Common extras: language proficiency (English, Cantonese, Mandarin); availability or notice period; expected salary when the posting asks for it ("state your current and expected salary"); visa status for non-permanent residents (for example IANG, GEP, Top Talent Pass).
- Dates: "Mar 2021 – Present".
- Notes: JobsDB, LinkedIn and CTgoodjobs are common; multinationals use Workday and similar ATS.

## CN — Mainland China
- Name: 简历 (résumé). Paper: A4. Length: 1 page for early-career, 2 for experienced.
- Language: Simplified Chinese for domestic employers; English or bilingual for foreign companies.
- Photo: commonly included for domestic employers; optional for foreign companies.
- Personal details: gender, age or date of birth, hometown and political status were traditionally listed and are now optional. Default: leave them out unless the user wants them.
- Common extras: a job-intention section (求职意向: target role, city, expected salary) near the top; university names are prominent.
- Dates: "2021.03 – 2023.06" or "2021.03 – 至今".
- Notes: BOSS直聘, 智联招聘, 前程无忧 and 猎聘 are common; ATS such as Moka and Beisen (北森) parse uploaded CVs.

## TW — Taiwan
- Name: 履歷 (résumé). Paper: A4. Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: Traditional Chinese for local employers; English for foreign companies.
- Photo: common for local employers; optional for foreign companies.
- Common extras: an autobiography (自傳) is often requested separately; military service status is sometimes listed by male candidates (only if the user wants it).
- Dates: "2021/03 – 2023/06" or "2021/03 – 迄今".
- Notes: 104人力銀行, 1111 and Cake profiles are widely used alongside uploaded CVs.

## SG — Singapore
- Name: CV or résumé. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages.
- Language: English, British spelling.
- Photo: not needed. Personal details: leave out age, race, religion and marital status (Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices). Never include the NRIC number.
- Common extras: citizenship or residency status (Singapore Citizen, PR, or requires an Employment Pass) matters for work-pass reasons; notice period and expected salary are commonly requested.
- Dates: "Mar 2021 – Present".

## MY — Malaysia
- Name: résumé or CV. Paper: A4. Length: 2–3 pages.
- Language: English for most professional roles; Malay for some local and government roles.
- Photo: still fairly common; optional.
- Personal details: never include the IC (MyKad) number.
- Common extras: expected salary and notice period.

## JP — Japan
- Japanese employers expect two documents: a 履歴書 (rirekisho), a standardized form with a photo, and a 職務経歴書 (shokumu keirekisho), a free-format career history on A4, usually 1–2 pages.
- This plugin produces the 職務経歴書 or an English résumé. It does not fill the rirekisho form; tell the user to complete it separately.
- Language: Japanese for Japanese employers; English résumé for foreign-affiliated firms (外資系).
- Common extras: 職務要約 (career summary) at the top; Japanese proficiency as a JLPT level for non-native speakers.
- Dates: Western years ("2021年4月") or the Japanese era; be consistent.

## KR — South Korea
- Many companies use their own application forms: 이력서 (résumé) plus 자기소개서 (self-introduction essay).
- Language: Korean for Korean employers; English résumé for foreign firms.
- Photo: still common at private companies; public institutions use blind hiring with no photo or personal details. Korean law restricts employers from collecting details such as hometown, marital status, family and physical information, so leave them out.
- Common extras: language test scores (TOEIC, OPIc) are often expected.

## AU — Australia / NZ — New Zealand
- Name: résumé or CV. Paper: A4. Length: 2–3 pages.
- Language: Australian or New Zealand English (British-style spelling).
- Photo: no. Personal details: no date of birth or age.
- Common extras: work rights (citizen, permanent resident, or visa subclass such as 482); a referees section with two referees, or "Referees available on request".
- Dates: "Mar 2021 – Present".

## IN — India
- Name: résumé or CV. Paper: A4. Length: 1 page for freshers, 2 for experienced.
- Language: English.
- Photo: optional. Personal details: date of birth and marital status were traditional; multinationals do not need them. Default: leave them out.
- Common extras: notice period (often 30–90 days) and current or expected CTC (for example "12 LPA") are frequently asked; include them only if the user wants.
- Notes: Naukri and LinkedIn are common.

## AE — United Arab Emirates, SA — Saudi Arabia, QA — Qatar (Gulf)
- Name: CV. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages.
- Language: English; Arabic for some local roles.
- Photo: common; optional.
- Common extras: nationality, visa status (residence visa, transferable, visit visa), notice period, driving licence (often relevant in the UAE).

## BR — Brazil / MX — Mexico / Latin America
- Name: currículo / currículum. Paper: A4 (Letter is also common in Mexico). Length: 1–2 pages.
- Language: Portuguese (Brazil) or Spanish, unless the posting is in English.
- Photo: optional.

## Generic default (any market not listed)
- Name: CV or résumé. Paper: A4. Length: 2 pages maximum (1 for early-career).
- Language: the posting's language. In English, follow the posting's spelling (US or UK).
- No photo and no personal details beyond contact information.
- Dates: "Mar 2021 – Present".
